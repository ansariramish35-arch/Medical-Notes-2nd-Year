## 1. Definitions

:::definition Standard definition
**Antibodies (immunoglobulins, Ig)** are glycoproteins of the **γ-globulin class** produced by plasma cells in response to an antigen, which **combine specifically** with the antigen that induced their formation.
:::

:::definition Antigen (for completeness)
Any substance that can **induce** an immune response (immunogen) and **react** with the products of that response. Hapten = reacts but cannot induce alone (needs a carrier).
:::

## 2. Basic Structure of Immunoglobulin (LAQ core)

All immunoglobulins share the same **H2L2 plan**:

- **Two identical heavy (H) chains** (≈50 kDa each) — determine the **class** (IgG = γ, IgA = α, IgM = μ, IgD = δ, IgE = ε).
- **Two identical light (L) chains** (≈25 kDa) — either **κ or λ** (never both in one molecule).
- Chains held by **inter-chain disulfide bonds**.
- Each chain folds into **domains**: one **variable (V)** domain at the N-terminus and 1–4 **constant (C)** domains. The V domains of one H + one L chain form the **antigen-binding site** → each Ig monomer is **bivalent**.
- **Hinge region** between CH1 and CH2 gives flexibility (richest in IgG3/IgA; IgM and IgE lack a hinge and use an extra CH domain instead).

### Enzymatic Fragments (classic exam item)

| Enzyme | Cleavage | Products | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Papain** | Above the hinge | **2 Fab + 1 Fc** | Fab binds antigen (univalent); Fc crystallizes |
| **Pepsin** | Below the hinge | **F(ab′)2 + pFc′** | F(ab′)2 remains **bivalent** — can still precipitate/agglutinate |

### Functions mapped to fragments

| Fragment | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **Fab** | Antigen recognition and binding (specificity of the antibody) |
| **Fc** | **All effector functions:** complement activation (IgG, IgM), binding to Fc receptors (opsonization, ADCC, mast cells via FcεRI), placental transfer (IgG), half-life regulation |

## 3. The Five Classes — Complete Comparison (Difference-question ready)

| Feature | IgG | IgM | IgA | IgD | IgE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy chain | γ | μ | α | δ | ε |
| Serum level | **Highest (≈75–80%)** | ≈5–10% | ≈10–15% | Trace | Trace |
| Structure | Monomer | **Pentamer** (J chain) | Monomer/dimer (secretory piece) | Monomer | Monomer |
| Molecular weight | ≈150 kDa | ≈900 kDa (largest) | ≈160–400 kDa | ≈185 kDa | ≈190 kDa |
| Complement fixation | Yes (IgG1, IgG3 best) | **Best (pentamer)** | No | No | No |
| Placental transfer | **Yes — the only class** | No | No | No | No |
| Half-life | ≈23 days (longest) | ≈5 days | ≈6 days | ≈3 days | ≈2 days |
| Order of response | Secondary response | **First antibody of primary response** | Mucosal | B-cell receptor | Allergy/parasites |
| Key function | Long-term immunity, opsonization | Primary response, ABO antibodies | **Mucosal/secretory immunity** | B-cell maturation receptor | Type I hypersensitivity, anti-helminth |

:::highyield One-liners per class (memorize)
- **IgG** — most abundant; only Ig to cross the placenta (passive immunity to the newborn); opsonin.
- **IgM** — first antibody in primary response; pentamer; best complement fixer; ABO isoagglutinins; fetal IgM ↑ = intrauterine infection (does not cross placenta, so fetal IgM is fetal in origin).
- **IgA** — secretory IgA (dimer + secretory piece) guards mucosae; dominant in colostrum, saliva, tears.
- **IgD** — surface receptor on naïve B cells with IgM.
- **IgE** — bound to mast cells/basophils via FcεRI; mediates anaphylaxis; raised in allergy and helminth infections.
:::

## 4. Antibody Functions (Enumeration — "Functions of immunoglobulins")

1. **Neutralization** — block toxin/virus attachment (antitoxin effect).
2. **Opsonization** — IgG-Fc + C3b coat microbes → phagocytosis via Fc/CR1 receptors.
3. **Complement activation** — classical pathway (IgM > IgG).
4. **ADCC** — antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (NK cells via FcγRIII; eosinophils against parasites via IgE).
5. **Agglutination and precipitation** — cross-linking particulate/soluble antigens.
6. **Mucosal immunity** — secretory IgA.
7. **Passive immunity** — transplacental IgG.
8. **Hypersensitivity mediation** — IgE (Type I), IgG/IgM (Types II, III).

## 6. Primary vs Secondary Immune Response

| Feature | Primary response | Secondary (anamnestic) response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lag phase | Long (5–10 days) | Short (1–3 days) |
| First antibody | **IgM** | **IgG** (class-switched) |
| Titre | Low | High |
| Affinity | Lower (unmatured) | Higher (affinity maturation) |
| Duration | Shorter | Longer |
| Basis | Naïve B cells | **Memory B cells** |

:::why WHY? — Why is the secondary response faster and stronger?
The first exposure generates **memory B cells** that already carry class-switched, affinity-matured receptors. On re-exposure they expand immediately, skip the lag, and secrete IgG in bulk — the scientific basis of **vaccination and boosters**.
:::

:::clinical Clinical correlation
- **Selective IgA deficiency** is the commonest primary immunodeficiency — recurrent sinopulmonary and GI infections, anaphylaxis risk with blood transfusion (anti-IgA antibodies).
- **Hyper-IgM syndrome**: defective class switching → high IgM, absent IgG/IgA.
- **Multiple myeloma** = monoclonal immunoglobulin (M band on electrophoresis) — a plasma-cell neoplasm.
:::
