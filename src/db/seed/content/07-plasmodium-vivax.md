## 1. Introduction and Classification

:::definition Exam definition
**Malaria** is an acute febrile illness caused by protozoa of the genus **Plasmodium**, transmitted by the bite of infected **female Anopheles mosquitoes**, characterized by cyclical fever paroxysms, anaemia and splenomegaly.
:::

- Phylum Apicomplexa → genus *Plasmodium*.
- Human species: **P. vivax** (benign tertian), **P. falciparum** (malignant tertian), **P. malariae** (quartan), **P. ovale** (ovale tertian), **P. knowlesi** (zoonotic, quotidian).

| Species | Fever periodicity | Cycle in RBC | Special feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P. vivax | 48 h — benign tertian | Prefers reticulocytes; Schüffner's dots; enlarged RBC | **Hypnozoites → relapses** |
| P. falciparum | 48 h — malignant tertian | All ages of RBC; multiple rings; banana gametocyte | Severe/cerebral malaria |
| P. malariae | 72 h — quartan | Band-form trophozoites | Nephrotic syndrome |
| P. ovale | 48 h | Oval RBCs with fimbriated edges | Hypnozoites |

**Hosts:** man = intermediate host (asexual cycle); female *Anopheles* = definitive host (sexual cycle — defined by where the sexual phase occurs).

## 2. Morphology — Diagnostic Stages in Blood

- **Ring (trophozoite):** RBC with delicate ring, chromatin dot; P. vivax shows single large ring in enlarged, pale RBC.
- **Schizont:** 12–24 merozoites + pigment (P. vivax ≈ 16).
- **Gametocytes:** round (vivax) vs banana/crescent (falciparum).
- **Schüffner's dots** (vivax/ovale) vs **Maurer's clefts** (falciparum).

## 3. Life Cycle — Complete (LAQ centrepiece)

{{figure:malaria-life-cycle}}

### Step-by-step narration

**A. In man (asexual schizogony)**
1. **Inoculation:** infected mosquito injects **sporozoites** (the **infective stage**) during a blood meal.
2. **Liver phase — exo-erythrocytic schizogony:** sporozoites invade hepatocytes → hepatic schizonts → rupture releases **thousands of merozoites** (≈8–12 days for vivax). In P. vivax/ovale, dormant forms called **hypnozoites** persist → cause **RELAPSES**.
3. **Erythrocytic phase:** merozoites invade RBCs → **ring → trophozoite → schizont** → RBC ruptures releasing merozoites + pigment → these invade fresh RBCs (cycle ≈ **48 h** in vivax).
4. **Fever paroxysm** coincides with synchronous RBC rupture.
5. Some merozoites differentiate into **gametocytes** (♂ microgametocytes, ♀ macrogametocytes) — the stage taken up by the mosquito.

**B. In mosquito (sexual cycle — sporogony)**
6. Mosquito ingests gametocytes → in the gut: exflagellation of microgamete → fertilization → **zygote → motile ookinete**.
7. Ookinete penetrates gut wall → **oocyst** → thousands of **sporozoites**.
8. Oocyst bursts; sporozoites reach **salivary glands** → cycle ready (sporogony ≈ 10–14 days at optimal temperature).

:::highyield Stage summary (memorize as a pair)
**Infective stage for man: sporozoite.** **Diagnostic stage: intra-erythrocytic parasites on blood smear.** Gametocytes = transmission stage to mosquito. Hypnozoites = relapse stage (vivax/ovale only).
:::

## 4. Pathogenesis and Clinical Features

```flow
Merozoites + malarial pigment released at RBC rupture
↓
Macrophages stimulated → TNF-α and IL-1 release
↓
Fever paroxysm: COLD stage (rigors) → HOT stage (high fever) → SWEATING stage (defervescence)
↓
Repeated cycles → haemolytic anaemia + splenomegaly
```

- **Classical paroxysm (vivax):** chill/rigor (15–60 min) → fever up to 40 °C (2–6 h) → profuse sweating → relief; repeats every **48 h**.
- **Anaemia:** RBC destruction + splenic removal of parasitized cells + marrow suppression.
- **Splenomegaly:** congested, hyperplastic spleen; chronic → tropical splenomegaly syndrome.
- **P. vivax is rarely fatal** (unlike falciparum); complications of falciparum: cerebral malaria, blackwater fever, ARDS, renal failure — RBCs of ALL ages infected, cytoadherence/rosetting cause microvascular sequestration.

:::why WHY? — Why does fever come in cycles?
Fever follows the **synchronous rupture of schizont-laden RBCs** releasing merozoites and pigment that trigger TNF/IL-1. Vivax/ovale/falciparum synchronize every 48 h (tertian); malariae every 72 h (quartan).
:::

## 5. Laboratory Diagnosis (Full Protocol)

{{figure:labdx-malaria}}

### 1. Specimen
Peripheral blood — ideally collected **during the rising fever/rigors, before antimalarial therapy**.

### 2. Films — the gold standard
| Film | How made | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thick smear** | Drop of blood, lysed (no fixation) | **Detection** — 20–40× concentration; most sensitive routine method |
| **Thin smear** | Fixed with methanol | **Species + stage identification** and parasitaemia count |

- Stain: **Giemsa** (Leishman/Field's also used; Field's stain for rapid field diagnosis).
- P. vivax findings: enlarged RBC, Schüffner's dots, single large rings, amoeboid trophozoites, 12–24 merozoite schizonts.

### 3. Rapid diagnostic tests (RDT)
- Immunochromatographic: **HRP-2** (P. falciparum-specific), **pLDH/aldolase** (pan-species). Result in ≈15 min; field-use.

### 4. Special methods
- **QBC** (quantitative buffy coat, acridine orange fluorescence).
- **PCR** — most sensitive; species confirmation, low parasitaemia.
- Serology (IFA, ELISA) — epidemiological surveys, blood-bank screening; NOT for acute diagnosis.

### 5. Diagnostic algorithm

```flow
Clinical suspicion (fever + chills + endemic exposure)
↓
Blood film: THICK (detect) + THIN (identify)
↓
Positive → report species, stage, parasitaemia → treat
↓
Negative but high suspicion → repeat film every 12–24 h × 3 / RDT / PCR
```

## 6. Treatment Principles (framework — follow national protocol for dosing)

| Goal | Drugs (classes) | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clinical cure — kill erythrocytic forms | **Chloroquine** (vivax, sensitive falciparum); ACTs for falciparum | Blood schizonticides stop the paroxysm |
| **Radical cure — kill hypnozoites** | **Primaquine** (14 days for vivax/ovale) | Prevents relapses; **check G6PD first** (haemolysis risk) |
| Gametocytocidal | Primaquine (falciparum gametocytes) | Blocks transmission |
| Prophylaxis | Chloroquine/mefloquine/atovaquone–proguanil (travellers) | Suppress erythrocytic stage |

:::why WHY? — Why must primaquine be added for P. vivax?
Chloroquine kills only **erythrocytic** stages. Vivax hides as **hypnozoites in the liver**, untouched by chloroquine; without primaquine the patient relapses. This two-drug logic ("clinical cure vs radical cure") is a favourite viva question.
:::

## 7. Prevention and Control (Enumeration)

1. **Mosquito control:** indoor residual spraying (IRS), larviciding, source reduction.
2. **Personal protection:** insecticide-treated bed nets (ITN/LLIN), repellents, protective clothing.
3. **Chemoprophylaxis** for travellers and pregnancy (per national policy).
4. **Case management:** early diagnosis + complete treatment to cut transmission.
5. **Surveillance and epidemic preparedness.**

:::clinical Clinical correlation
A returning traveller with cyclical "cold–hot–sweating" fever after 10–15 days: think malaria until proven otherwise. A **single negative film does not exclude malaria** — repeat smears before clearing the diagnosis. In pregnancy and children in endemic zones, malarial anaemia is a leading presentation.
