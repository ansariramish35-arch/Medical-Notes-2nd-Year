## 1. Definitions

:::definition Neoplasm (Willis)
"A neoplasm is an **abnormal mass of tissue**, the growth of which **exceeds and is uncoordinated** with that of the normal surrounding tissues, and **persists in the same excessive manner after cessation of the stimulus** which evoked the change."
:::

- **Benign tumour:** localized, non-invasive, **never metastasizes**; suffix **-oma**.
- **Malignant tumour (cancer):** invasive and capable of **metastasis**; carcinomas (epithelial) and sarcomas (mesenchymal).
- **Anaplasia** = loss of differentiation (hallmark of malignancy); **dysplasia** = disordered growth (potentially reversible, pre-neoplastic); **metaplasia** = reversible replacement of one adult cell type by another.

## 4. Routes of Metastasis (LAQ Part B)

1. **Lymphatic spread** — typical of **carcinomas**: tumour cells enter lymphatics → regional nodes (breast → axillary; stomach → may reach left supraclavicular **Virchow node**). Nodes may initially act as filters.
2. **Haematogenous spread** — typical of **sarcomas** (and late carcinomas): veins are thin-walled and easily invaded. **Liver and lungs** are the most common secondary sites (all portal drainage → liver; all caval drainage → lungs). Bone, brain, adrenals follow.
3. **Direct seeding of body cavities** — e.g. gastric carcinoma through the wall → peritoneal implants → ovarian deposit = **Krukenberg tumour**; ovarian carcinoma → peritoneal carcinomatosis.

:::highyield The rule and its exceptions
Carcinomas → lymphatics first; sarcomas → blood first. **Exceptions:** renal cell carcinoma (renal vein invasion), hepatocellular carcinoma, follicular carcinoma of thyroid, choriocarcinoma — all invade **veins early**.
:::

## 5. Characteristics of Malignant Cells (SN support)

- **Anaplasia:** pleomorphism, hyperchromatic large nuclei, ↑ nuclear:cytoplasmic ratio (1:1 instead of 1:4), prominent nucleoli, atypical mitoses, loss of polarity.
- **Cancer cachexia:** wasting with anorexia and anemia — cytokine-mediated (TNF-α/cachexin), not simply due to nutritional drain.
- **Paraneoplastic syndromes:** remote effects not explained by metastasis — e.g. Cushing (ectopic ACTH — small cell lung Ca), hypercalcaemia (PTHrP — squamous Ca), Trousseau syndrome (migratory thrombophlebitis).

## 6. Carcinogenesis — Molecular Basis (Level 2)

| Gene class | Normal role | Cancer change | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Proto-oncogenes** | Promote growth | Gain-of-function → **oncogene** (dominant) | RAS, MYC, HER2, BCR-ABL |
| **Tumour suppressors** | Brake the cycle | Loss of both alleles (two-hit) | **RB** (retinoblastoma), **p53** ("guardian of the genome"), APC, BRCA1/2 |
| DNA repair genes | Fix mutations | Inactivation → mutator phenotype | HNPCC mismatch-repair genes, xeroderma pigmentosum |
| Apoptosis regulators | Remove damaged cells | BCL-2 overexpression | Follicular lymphoma t(14;18) |

**p53:** activated by DNA damage → arrests the cycle (via p21) for repair, or triggers apoptosis if damage is irreparable. Mutated in >50% of human cancers.

## 8. High-Yield and Exam Framework

:::highyield High-yield one-liners
- **Metastasis is the single most reliable criterion of malignancy**; benign tumours never metastasize.
- Carcinomas → lymphatics; sarcomas → blood; exceptions memorized.
- Liver + lungs = commonest metastatic sites.
- p53 = guardian of the genome; RB = first tumour suppressor discovered (retinoblastoma, two-hit).
- Krukenberg tumour = mucin-secreting signet-ring deposit in ovary (usually stomach primary).
:::

**LAQ framework:** Willis definition → terminology table → benign vs malignant table → metastasis definition + three routes + diagram → characteristics of malignancy → one paragraph molecular basis → tumour markers → conclusion.
