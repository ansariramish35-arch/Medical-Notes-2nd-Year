## 1. Definitions

:::definition Standard definition
**Inflammation** is the **protective response of vascularized living tissue to injury**, involving cells, blood vessels and soluble mediators, whose purpose is to **eliminate the cause of injury and initiate repair**.
:::

:::definition Acute inflammation
**Acute inflammation** is the **early, rapid** response (minutes to days) dominated by **vascular changes, fluid exudation and neutrophil emigration**.
:::

:::source Cardinal signs (Celsus + Virchow) — the classic SAQ
**RUBOR** (redness) · **TUMOR** (swelling) · **CALOR** (heat) · **DOLOR** (pain) · **FUNCTIO LAESA** (loss of function, added by Virchow).
:::

## 2. Components of the Response (the three-part plan)

1. **Vascular changes** — alter blood flow.
2. **Exudation** — fluid and proteins leave the vessel.
3. **Cellular events** — leucocytes leave the vessel and destroy the offender.

## 3. Vascular Events (Pathogenesis — Part A)

```flow
Transient arteriolar vasoconstriction (seconds; neurogenic)
↓
ARTERIOLAR VASODILATATION (histamine, NO, prostaglandins) → ↑ blood flow → RUBOR + CALOR
↓
INCREASED VASCULAR PERMEABILITY (histamine, bradykinin, leukotrienes, VEGF) → protein-rich EXUDATE escapes → TUMOR
↑
Exudate dilutes toxins, brings antibodies and complement, fibrin walls off the focus
↓
Concentrated blood → stasis → leucocyte MARGINATION
```

- **Transudate vs exudate** (classic difference):

| Feature | Transudate | Exudate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protein | Low (<3 g/dL) | High (>3 g/dL) |
| Specific gravity | <1.012 | >1.012 |
| Cells | Few | Many (neutrophils) |
| Mechanism | ↑ hydrostatic / ↓ oncotic pressure | **↑ permeability** (inflammation) |
| Example | Oedema of heart failure | Inflammatory pleural effusion |

- DOLOR from prostaglandins (sensitize) and bradykinin; FUNCTIO LAESA from pain + swelling.

## 4. Cellular Events (Pathogenesis — Part B)

```flow
MARGINATION (cells pushed to vessel wall in stasis)
↓
ROLLING — loose, selectin-mediated (E-selectin, P-selectin, L-selectin)
↓
FIRM ADHESION — integrins (CD11/CD18 on leucocyte ↔ ICAM-1/VCAM-1 on endothelium)
↓
TRANSMIGRATION (diapedesis) through endothelial junctions (PECAM-1/CD31)
↓
CHEMOTAXIS — migration up a chemical gradient: C5a, IL-8, LTB4, bacterial peptides (fMLP)
↓
PHAGOCYTOSIS — recognition → engulfment → killing
```

### Phagocytosis in detail (Level 2)
1. **Recognition & attachment** — via **opsonins** (IgG-Fc and **C3b**) binding leucocyte receptors; unopsonized particles are poorly ingested.
2. **Engulfment** — pseudopods enclose the particle → **phagosome** → fuses with lysosome → **phagolysosome**.
3. **Killing:**
   - **Oxygen-dependent (main):** **respiratory burst** — NADPH oxidase → superoxide → **H2O2**; **myeloperoxidase (MPO) + halide → HOCl** = the most potent bactericidal system.
   - **Oxygen-independent:** lysozyme, lactoferrin (iron chelation), defensins, low pH.

:::why WHY? — Why does chronic granulomatous disease cause recurrent infections?
Defective NADPH oxidase → **no respiratory burst** → phagocytes can ingest but cannot kill catalase-positive organisms (Staphylococcus, Aspergillus). The H2O2–MPO–halide system is the cell's main weapon; without it, ingested bacteria survive inside the phagocyte.
:::

## 5. Chemical Mediators (Enumeration — high-yield)

| Mediator | Source | Main actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Histamine** | Mast cells, basophils, platelets | Vasodilation, ↑ permeability (immediate phase) |
| Serotonin | Platelets | Same as histamine |
| Prostaglandins (PGE2, PGI2, PGD2) | Arachidonate via COX | Vasodilation, pain, fever |
| **Leukotrienes (C4, D4, E4)** | Arachidonate via LOX | Sustained ↑ permeability, bronchospasm |
| **Bradykinin** | Kinin system | Pain, ↑ permeability, vasodilation |
| **C3a, C5a** | Complement | Anaphylatoxins; **C5a also chemotactic** |
| **IL-1, TNF** | Macrophages | Endothelial activation, fever, systemic effects |
| **IL-8** | Macrophages, endothelium | Neutrophil chemotaxis + activation |
| NO | Endothelium, macrophages | Vasodilation, microbial killing |

## 6. Outcomes of Acute Inflammation (Enumeration — mandatory)

1. **Resolution** — complete return to normal (mild injury, intact framework).
2. **Suppuration (abscess)** — persistent pyogenic infection; walling-off of pus.
3. **Organization / fibrosis** — exudate replaced by granulation tissue → scar.
4. **Chronic inflammation** — persistent agent.
5. **Systemic spread** — bacteraemia → septicaemia, toxaemia, pyaemia.

:::clinical Clinical correlation
**Appendicitis** is the bedside model of this whole chapter: vascular engorgement (rubor of the serosa), exudate into the wall and lumen, neutrophil infiltration, pain from mediators, and the possible outcomes — resolution (rare), abscess, perforation with peritonitis, or organization.
:::

## 7. Systemic Effects (SAQ support)
Fever (IL-1/TNF → prostaglandins in hypothalamus), leucocytosis (IL-1, TNF), ↑ ESR/CRP (acute-phase proteins), malaise; severe sepsis → septic shock.

## 8. High-Yield and Exam Framework

:::highyield High-yield one-liners
- Acute = **neutrophils**; chronic = macrophages/lymphocytes (the single most-quoted distinction).
- **Histamine** opens the immediate permeability gap (venules); leukotrienes sustain it.
- Chemotaxis: **C5a, IL-8, LTB4**.
- Opsonins: **IgG-Fc and C3b**.
- Main killing system: **H2O2–MPO–halide** (oxygen-dependent).
- Cardinal signs: RUBOR · TUMOR · CALOR · DOLOR · FUNCTIO LAESA.
:::

**LAQ framework:** definition + cardinal signs → vascular flowchart → exudate table → cellular flowchart → phagocytosis steps → mediators table → outcomes enumeration → systemic effects → conclusion (inflammation is protective but can damage — example: ARDS).
