## 1. Definition

:::definition Exam definition
**Complement** is a system of **heat-labile serum proteins (about 30)** synthesized mainly by the liver, which act in an **enzymatic cascade** to mediate lysis of microbes, opsonization, inflammation and immune-complex clearance.
:::

- Named C1–C9 (classical-pathway discovery order; C4 was discovered before C2 — hence the numbering).
- **Heat-labile:** inactivated at **56 °C for 30 minutes** (classic exam fact).
- Activated components are suffixed "a"/"b" (C3a = small anaphylatoxin fragment; C3b = large binding fragment).

## 2. The Three Activation Pathways (Enumeration)

| Pathway | Initiated by | C3 convertase | Key feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Classical** | Antigen–antibody complexes (IgG, IgM) | **C4b2a** | Part of humoral immunity |
| **Alternative** | Microbial surfaces (LPS, zymosan) directly | **C3bBb** | Innate; no antibody needed |
| **Lectin (MBL)** | Mannose-binding lectin on microbial sugars | C4b2a (same as classical) | Antibody-independent |

All three converge on **C3** and share the identical terminal sequence **C5 → C9 (MAC)**.

## 3. Classical Pathway — Step-by-step (LAQ)

### Level 1 — One-line mechanism
Antibody bound to antigen recruits and activates **C1**, which builds the **C3 convertase (C4b2a)**; C3b amplifies the cascade and with C5 convertase assembles the **membrane attack complex (C5b–9)** that punches osmotic pores in the target membrane.

### Level 2 — Step-by-step sequence

1. **Recognition:** IgG (IgG1, IgG3) or IgM binds antigen; **C1q** binds to exposed Fc regions (≥2 Fc needed — hence IgM pentamer is most efficient).
2. **Activation:** C1q engagement activates **C1r → C1s** (serine protease).
3. **Early cleavage:** C1s cleaves **C4 → C4a + C4b** and **C2 → C2a + C2b**; C4b and C2a assemble on the membrane as **C4b2a = C3 convertase**.
4. **Central step:** C3 convertase cleaves **C3 → C3a + C3b** (the biggest amplification step).
5. **C5 convertase:** C3b joins C4b2a → **C4b2a3b**, which cleaves C5.
6. **Terminal pathway:** **C5b** recruits C6, C7, C8 and polymerizes 12–18 copies of **C9** → transmembrane **pore (MAC)** → osmotic lysis.

### Level 3 — Important mechanistic detail

- **C3a and C5a are anaphylatoxins:** mast-cell degranulation, vasodilation, ↑ permeability; **C5a is also the most powerful chemotactic factor** for neutrophils.
- **C3b is the great opsonin** — phagocytes carry CR1 (C3b receptors).
- **Regulation prevents self-damage:** C1 inhibitor (C1-INH) blocks C1r/C1s; **DAF (CD55)** and **MCP (CD46)** accelerate decay of convertases; **CD59 (protectin)** blocks C9 polymerization; **Factor H and I** control the alternative pathway.

:::clinical Clinical correlations
- **C1-INH deficiency → hereditary angioedema** (uncontrolled bradykinin/complement activation).
- **C3 deficiency** → severe recurrent pyogenic infections (the central node is gone).
- **Terminal component (C5–C9) deficiency → recurrent Neisseria infections** (meningococcal/gonococcal) — the MAC is the only anti-Neisseria weapon.
- **C2/C4 deficiency** → SLE-like immune-complex disease (poor complex clearance).
- **PNH** (deficient GPI anchors → no DAF/CD59) → complement-mediated haemolysis.
:::

## 4. Functions of Complement (SAQ — "Enumerate the functions")

1. **Lysis** of cells and microbes (MAC, C5b–9).
2. **Opsonization** (C3b, iC3b) → enhanced phagocytosis.
3. **Inflammation** — anaphylatoxins C3a, C4a, C5a.
4. **Chemotaxis** — C5a (and C5b67) attracts neutrophils.
5. **Immune-complex clearance** — C3b-coated complexes bind CR1 on erythrocytes → carried to liver/spleen.
6. **Enhancement of antibody responses** (C3d with antigen engages CR2/CD21 on B cells).

## 5. Measurement and Lab Correlation

| Test | Measures |
| --- | --- |
| **CH50** | Total classical-pathway haemolytic activity (C1–C9) |
| **AH50** | Alternative-pathway activity |
| Individual component assays | C3, C4 levels by immunodiffusion/nephelometry |

**Pattern reading (exam favourite):** low C3 AND C4 → classical-pathway consumption (SLE); low C3 with normal C4 → alternative pathway (post-infectious GN).

## 6. Comparison — Classical vs Alternative Pathway

| Feature | Classical | Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initiation | Antigen–antibody complex | Microbial surfaces directly |
| Antibody required | Yes (IgG/IgM) | No |
| Components | C1, C4, C2, C3… | C3, Factor B, Factor D, properdin |
| C3 convertase | C4b2a | C3bBb |
| Speed | Slower (needs Ab) | Immediate (innate) |
| Stabilizer | — | **Properdin** |

:::highyield High-yield one-liners
- Complement = heat-labile, inactivated at **56 °C × 30 min**.
- **C3 convertases:** classical **C4b2a**, alternative **C3bBb** — never mix them up.
- **C3b** = opsonin; **C3a/C5a** = anaphylatoxins; **C5a** = chemotaxin; **C5b–9** = MAC.
- All pathways **converge at C3**.
- MAC deficiency → **Neisseria**; C3 deficiency → pyogenic infections; C1-INH deficiency → angioedema.
:::
