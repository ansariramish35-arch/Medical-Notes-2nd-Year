## 1. Definitions

:::definition Cell injury
**Cell injury** is the state in which a cell fails to maintain homeostasis under physiological stress or pathological stimulus. It is **reversible** up to a point; beyond the "point of no return" (mitochondrial inability to recover oxidative phosphorylation + profound membrane damage) it progresses to **cell death**.
:::

:::definition Necrosis
**Necrosis** is **pathological** cell death in a living organism, characterized by enzymatic digestion and protein denaturation, **always accompanied by inflammation**.
:::

:::definition Apoptosis
**Apoptosis** is **programmed** cell death — an energy-dependent, genetically controlled dismantling of the cell into membrane-bound **apoptotic bodies** that are phagocytosed **without inflammation**.
:::

## 2. Causes and Mechanisms of Cell Injury (Enumeration + pathway)

**Causes:** hypoxia/ischaemia (commonest), physical agents, chemicals and drugs, infectious agents, immunological reactions, genetic defects, nutritional derangements, ageing.

**Four principal biochemical mechanisms:**
1. **ATP depletion** — failure of Na+/K+ pump → cell swelling (hydropic change); failure of Ca2+ pumps.
2. **Mitochondrial damage** — loss of oxidative phosphorylation; mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening.
3. **Influx of calcium** — activates phospholipases, proteases, endonucleases, ATPases.
4. **Oxidative stress** — free radicals (ROS) peroxidize lipids, damage proteins and DNA.

```flow
Ischaemia / hypoxia / toxin
↓
↓ Oxidative phosphorylation → ↓ ATP
↓
Na+/K+ pump failure → Na+ + water influx → CELLULAR SWELLING (hydropic change — first visible change)
↓
Ca2+ pump failure → ↑ intracellular Ca2+ → enzyme activation
↓
Membrane damage + mitochondrial failure = POINT OF NO RETURN
↓
NECROSIS (swelling pathway) or APOPTOSIS (programmed pathway)
```

**Reversible vs irreversible — the exam table:**

| Feature | Reversible injury | Irreversible injury |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swelling | Cellular + organ swelling | Marked membrane blebbing |
| Membrane | Intact | **Loss of integrity (key event)** |
| Mitochondria | Swollen | Amorphous densities, no ATP recovery |
| Nucleus | Chromatin clumping | Pyknosis → karyorrhexis → karyolysis |
| Ribosomes | Detachment (↓ protein synthesis) | — |
| Outcome | Recovery if stimulus removed | Cell death |

## 3. Necrosis — Morphology and Types

### 3.1 What happens (morphogenesis)
- **Cytoplasm:** increased eosinophilia (loss of RNA), glassy appearance, vacuolation, calcification.
- **Nucleus:** **pyknosis** (shrunken, dense) → **karyorrhexis** (fragmentation) → **karyolysis** (enzymatic fading). The nuclear sequence is the microscopic hallmark.
- The dead cell is recognized as a **"ghost outline"** — architecture preserved but no nucleus.

### 3.2 Types of Necrosis (Complete classification)

| Type | Mechanism | Typical sites / examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Coagulative** | Protein denaturation dominates over enzymolysis; architecture preserved days | **Ischaemic infarcts of all solid organs except brain** (kidney, heart, spleen) |
| **Liquefactive** | Enzymatic digestion dominates | **Brain infarcts**, abscesses, pancreatitis (with fat necrosis) |
| **Caseous** | Combination — friable "cheese-like" | **Tuberculosis**, some fungi |
| **Fat necrosis** | Lipase digestion of fat → chalky white (saponification with Ca2+) | Acute pancreatitis (enzymatic), breast trauma (traumatic) |
| **Fibrinoid** | Immune complexes + fibrin in vessel walls | Malignant hypertension, vasculitis |
| **Gangrenous** | Not a distinct pattern — coagulative necrosis + putrefaction of a limb/bowel | Dry (ischaemic), wet (+ infection), gas (Clostridium) |

### 3.3 Gross vs microscopic recognition

| Feature | Gross | Microscopic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coagulative | Pale, firm, wedge-shaped (infarct) | Ghost outlines, no nuclei, preserved architecture |
| Liquefactive | Soft, cystic cavity / creamy pus | Amorphous debris, neutrophils |
| Caseous | Yellow-white, friable, cheese-like | Structureless granular debris + granulomatous rim |

:::clinical Clinical correlation
**Myocardial infarction:** coagulative necrosis at 12–24 h → neutrophilic infiltration day 1–3 → macrophage removal week 1–3 → granulation tissue → scar. The timing of neutrophils/macrophages explains **rupture risk at days 3–7** — an examiner's favourite correlation.
:::

## 4. Apoptosis — Mechanism and Morphology

{{figure:necrosis-apoptosis}}

### Two trigger pathways (Level-2 mechanism)
1. **Intrinsic (mitochondrial):** cell stress (DNA damage, growth-factor withdrawal) → **p53** → pro-apoptotic **BAX/BAK** oppose BCL-2 → cytochrome c release → **caspase-9** activation.
2. **Extrinsic (death-receptor):** FasL–Fas or TNF receptor engagement → **caspase-8** activation.
Both converge on **executioner caspases (caspase-3/6)** → controlled dismantling; apoptotic bodies are cleared by macrophages before they leak.

**Physiological apoptosis:** embryogenesis (interdigital webs), hormone-dependent involution (endometrium), deletion of self-reactive lymphocytes, epithelial turnover.
**Pathological apoptosis:** viral hepatitis (Councilman bodies), graft rejection, tumour regression, radiation injury.

## 5. Necrosis vs Apoptosis — Complete Difference Table

| Feature | Necrosis | Apoptosis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Always pathological | Physiological or pathological |
| Cell size | Enlarged (swelling) | **Shrunken** |
| Nucleus | Pyknosis → karyorrhexis → karyolysis | Fragmentation into nucleosome-sized pieces |
| Plasma membrane | **Disrupted** | Intact, blebs, altered orientation (phosphatidylserine flip) |
| Cellular contents | Enzymatic digestion; leak out | Packaged into **apoptotic bodies** |
| Inflammation | **Always present** | **Absent** |
| Energy | Passive (ATP-independent) | Active, **ATP-dependent** |
| Mechanism | Uncontrolled enzymolysis | **Caspase cascade**, gene-programmed |

## 6. High-Yield and Exam Framework

:::highyield High-yield one-liners
- First manifestation of reversible injury = **cellular swelling (hydropic change)**.
- Point of no return = **membrane damage + mitochondrial failure**.
- Necrosis = inflammation always; apoptosis = never.
- **Coagulative** = ischaemia (all except brain); **liquefactive** = brain + abscess; **caseous** = TB.
- Apoptosis is driven by **caspases**; anti-apoptotic = **BCL-2**, pro-apoptotic = **BAX**, sensor = **p53**.
:::

**LAQ framework:** definitions → causes enumeration → mechanism flowchart → reversible/irreversible table → necrosis (morphogenesis + types table) → apoptosis (two pathways) → the master comparison table → conclusion.
