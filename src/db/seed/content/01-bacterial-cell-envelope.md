## 1. Definition

:::definition Standard definition
Bacteria are **unicellular prokaryotic microorganisms** lacking a true nucleus (DNA is a single circular chromosome condensed as a **nucleoid**), lacking membrane-bound organelles, and possessing **70S ribosomes**. Almost all bacteria are bounded by a rigid **cell wall** containing **peptidoglycan** (exception: Mycoplasma — no cell wall).
:::

:::definition Simple understanding
Think of the bacterium as a factory: the **nucleoid** is the master blueprint, **ribosomes** are the assembly lines, the **plasma membrane** is the factory gate, the **cell wall** is the protective wall around the campus, and the **capsule, flagella and pili** are optional add-ons fitted only to some models.
:::

## 2. Structural Classification of Bacterial Components

| Category | Structure | Function in one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Essential** (all bacteria) | Nucleoid | Genetic information (single circular dsDNA) |
| | Cytoplasm + 70S ribosomes | Protein synthesis (30S + 50S subunits) |
| | Plasma membrane | Selective permeability, respiration enzymes, septum formation |
| | Cell wall (except Mycoplasma) | Shape, rigidity, osmotic protection |
| **Accessory** (some bacteria) | Capsule / slime layer | Antiphagocytic; adhesion |

| | Mesosome | Membrane invagination (largely an artifact) |

## 3. The Cell Envelope in Detail

The envelope = everything outside the cytoplasm: **capsule (optional) → cell wall → plasma membrane**.

### 3.1 Capsule

- Polysaccharide coat (polypeptide — poly-D-glutamic acid — in **Bacillus anthracis**).
- **Function:** antiphagocytic (major virulence factor), adhesion, resistance to desiccation.
- **Quellung reaction** (capsule swelling) demonstrates capsule microscopically.
- Encapsulated organisms worth memorizing: *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Cryptococcus neoformans* (fungal).

### 3.2 Cell Wall

:::definition Exam definition
The bacterial cell wall is a rigid structure **outside the plasma membrane**, composed of **peptidoglycan (murein)**, responsible for the characteristic shape of the bacterium and for protecting it against **osmotic lysis**.
:::

**Peptidoglycan chemistry (the part examiners love):**

1. Linear glycan backbone of alternating **N-acetylglucosamine (NAG)** and **N-acetylmuramic acid (NAM)**.
2. A **tetrapeptide side chain** (L-alanine → D-glutamic acid → meso-diaminopimelic acid → D-alanine in Gram-negative; the 3rd residue is **L-lysine** in many Gram-positives) hangs from NAM.
3. **Transpeptidation** cross-links the side chains — this is the step blocked by **β-lactam antibiotics** (which bind penicillin-binding proteins, PBPs).
4. **Lysozyme** (tears, saliva) cleaves the β-1,4 bond between NAG and NAM.

### 3.3 Gram-Positive Cell Wall

- **Thick peptidoglycan (20–80 nm)**, up to 40 layers, about 50% of wall dry weight.
- **Teichoic acids** (wall teichoic + membrane-anchored **lipoteichoic acid**) — antigenic, adhesion; the "Gram-positive antigen".
- **No outer membrane, no LPS, no periplasmic space.**
- Result: retains crystal violet–iodine complex → **purple/violet**.

### 3.4 Gram-Negative Cell Wall

- **Thin peptidoglycan (2–3 nm)**, 1–2 layers, about 10% of wall.
- **Periplasmic space** between membranes contains hydrolytic enzymes and β-lactamases.
- **Outer membrane** unique to Gram-negatives:
  - Inner leaflet = phospholipid; outer leaflet = **lipopolysaccharide (LPS)**.
  - **LPS = lipid A (endotoxin, the toxic moiety) + core polysaccharide + O-specific antigen** (serotyping basis).
  - **Porins** (e.g. OmpF/OmpC) — channels for small molecules; mutations cause drug resistance.
- Result: decolorized, takes up safranin → **pink/red**.

### 3.5 Plasma Membrane

- Phospholipid bilayer **without sterols** (exception: Mycoplasma incorporates cholesterol).
- Houses respiratory-chain enzymes (functional equivalent of mitochondria), permeases, and the machinery for wall synthesis and septation.

## 4. Gram Staining — Principle and Procedure

:::source Source question wording
"Describe the principle and procedure of Gram staining with a labelled diagram." — answer as **Principle → Procedure → Result → Interpretation**.
:::

**Principle:** Crystal violet and Gram's iodine form a large **CV–I complex** inside the cell. In Gram-positives the **thick, highly cross-linked peptidoglycan** traps the complex even when the decolorizer shrinks the wall; in Gram-negatives the **outer membrane is dissolved by alcohol** and the thin peptidoglycan cannot retain the complex, which is washed out, leaving the cell to take the counterstain.

```flow
Smear fixed by heat
↓
Crystal violet 60 s (PRIMARY stain — all cells purple)
↓
Gram's iodine 60 s (MORDANT — CV–I complex formed)
↓
Alcohol / acetone 10–20 s (DECOLORIZER — the critical step)
↓
Safranin 30 s (COUNTERSTAIN)
↓
Gram-positive = PURPLE · Gram-negative = PINK/RED
```

| Reagent | Role | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crystal violet | Primary stain | All cells stain purple |
| Gram's iodine | Mordant | Fixes dye as CV–I complex |
| Alcohol/acetone | **Decolorizer (critical step)** | Removes CV–I from Gram-negatives only |
| Safranin | Counterstain | Colorless Gram-negatives stain pink |

:::warning Common exam errors
**Over-decolorization** → Gram-positives appear falsely Gram-negative. **Under-decolorization** → Gram-negatives appear falsely positive. Old cultures lose Gram positivity. These three statements are classic short-question traps.
:::

## 5. Surface Appendages

### 5.1 Flagella

- Long protein (flagellin) filaments; **organelles of motility**.
- Arrangement is taxonomically useful:

| Pattern | Term | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single polar | Monotrichous | *Vibrio cholerae* |
| Tuft at one pole | Lophotrichous | *Spirillum* |
| Both poles | Amphitrichous | *Alcaligenes* |
| All over | Peritrichous | *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *Proteus* |

- **H antigen** = flagellar antigen; basis of serotyping (Kauffmann–White scheme for Salmonella).

### 5.2 Pili (Fimbriae)

- Short, straight, hair-like; protein **pilin**.
- **Common pili** = adhesion to host cells (colonization factors of ETEC, gonococci).
- **Sex pilus** (F pilus) = conjugation bridge for DNA transfer.

### 5.3 Spore (Endospore)

:::definition Exam definition
A spore is a **highly resistant dormant intracellular structure** formed by some Gram-positive bacilli (*Bacillus*, *Clostridium*) under nutrient deprivation; it is a mechanism of **survival, NOT reproduction** (one cell → one spore → one cell).
:::

- Resistance due to: **dipicolinic acid + calcium**, dehydrated core, thick cortex and coat.
- Killed only by proper **autoclaving (121 °C, 15 min, 15 psi)** — the reference sterilization standard.
- Spore position is diagnostically useful: central, subterminal or terminal.

## 6. Gram-Positive vs Gram-Negative — Complete Comparison

| Feature | Gram-positive | Gram-negative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peptidoglycan | Thick, multilayered (20–80 nm) | Thin, 1–2 layers (2–3 nm) |
| Teichoic acids | Present | Absent |
| Outer membrane | Absent | **Present** |
| LPS / endotoxin | Absent | **Present (lipid A)** |
| Periplasmic space | Absent | Present |
| Porins | Absent | Present |
| Colour after Gram stain | Purple/violet | Pink/red |
| Susceptibility to penicillin | Generally more susceptible | Less (outer membrane barrier) |
| Susceptibility to lysozyme | Susceptible | Relatively resistant |
| Exotoxin production | Common (many species) | Less common (but endotoxin always present) |
| Examples | Staphylococci, Streptococci, Clostridia | E. coli, Salmonella, Neisseria |

:::why WHY? — Why do Gram-negatives resist many antibiotics?
The outer membrane acts as a **permeability barrier** (large or hydrophobic molecules cannot pass; only small hydrophilic drugs enter via porins) and the periplasm concentrates **β-lactamases** near their target, destroying the drug before it reaches PBPs. Hence many Gram-negatives are intrinsically resistant to penicillin G.
:::

## 7. Clinical Correlation

- **Endotoxin (lipid A)** is released on Gram-negative lysis → fever (IL-1, TNF via macrophages), hypotension, DIC → **septic/endotoxic shock**. Meningococcaemia is the classic exam example.
- **Capsule**: antiphagocytic → encapsulated bacteria dominate in **asplenic patients** and in paediatric meningitis (S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, N. meningitidis).
- **Plasmids** carry resistance genes (R factors) → spread by **conjugation** through the sex pilus.

:::clinical Clinical correlation chain
Gram-negative septicaemia → LPS (lipid A) binds CD14/TLR-4 on macrophages → TNF-α + IL-1 release → fever, vasodilation, capillary leak → **septic shock with DIC** (Waterhouse–Friderichsen syndrome in meningococcaemia). This single chain answers "why is endotoxin dangerous".
:::

## 8. University LAQ Answer Framework

**Question: "Describe the structure of the bacterial cell. Add a note on the Gram-positive and Gram-negative cell walls and the principle of Gram staining."**

1. **Introduction** — bacteria are prokaryotes; state the plan of the answer.
2. **General structure** — draw and label the bacterial cell (Figure 1.1).
3. **Essential vs accessory structures** — table in section 2.
4. **Cell wall chemistry** — NAG/NAM/cross-linking; β-lactam and lysozyme targets.
5. **Gram-positive vs Gram-negative wall** — draw the side-by-side envelope diagram.
6. **Gram stain principle + procedure** — the four-reagent flow.
7. **Significance** — classification guide, therapeutic guide, endotoxin concept.
8. **Conclusion** — the wall explains staining, susceptibility and virulence.

:::exam Must-use exam keywords
prokaryote · peptidoglycan · NAG–NAM · transpeptidation · teichoic acid · outer membrane · LPS · lipid A · endotoxin · periplasm · porins · crystal violet–iodine complex · decolorizer
:::

:::highyield High-yield one-liners
- The **decolorizer** is the critical step of Gram staining.
- **Lipid A** is the toxic part of LPS; the **O antigen** is the serotyping part.
- **Mycoplasma** = the bacterium without a cell wall (does not Gram-stain; β-lactams ineffective).
- Spore = survival, **never** reproduction.
- Penicillins act on **PBPs** and kill by **autolysin-mediated lysis**.
:::
