## 1. Introduction and Classification

:::definition Exam definition
**Ascaris lumbricoides** (the large intestinal roundworm) is the largest nematode parasitizing the human small intestine; infection (**ascariasis**) is acquired by ingesting **embryonated eggs** from contaminated soil.
:::

- Phylum Nematoda (roundworms — cylindrical, unsegmented, dioecious).
- **Habitat:** lumen of jejunum/ileum. One of the **soil-transmitted helminths** (with hookworm, Trichuris).
- Life span of adult ≈ 1 year; no multiplication inside the host.

## 2. Morphology — Stage by Stage

| Stage | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Adult** | Cream-white, cylindrical; ♀ 20–35 cm (straight tail), ♂ 15–30 cm (ventrally curved tail); mouth with three lips |
| **Fertilized egg (diagnostic)** | Oval, 45–75 µm, thick shell, **mamillated (bumpy) albuminous coat**, golden-brown, unsegmented ovum inside |
| **Unfertilized egg** | Longer, narrower, irregular, no true shell layers |
| **Larva** | Rhabditiform → after lung migration becomes infective within egg; hatches as L2/L3 in intestine |

:::highyield Recognition checklist for the lab
Fertilized egg = "mamillated coat + unsegmented ovum". If asked to identify a stool egg image: mamillated coat is the key visual clue; absence of segmentation distinguishes it from hookworm eggs (which are segmented/cleaving).
:::

## 3. Complete Life Cycle

{{figure:ascaris-life-cycle}}

**Step-by-step:**
1. **Unembryonated eggs** passed in faeces.
2. In warm moist soil, the egg embryonates over **2–3 weeks** → **embryonated (infective) egg** containing the larva.
3. Ingestion with contaminated vegetables/water (especially children, pica).
4. Larvae hatch in the **small intestine**, penetrate the mucosa → **portal circulation → liver → heart → lungs** (≈4–7 days).
5. In the **alveoli** the larvae moult, ascend the **bronchi → trachea → pharynx**, are coughed up and **swallowed**.
6. Back in the small intestine they mature to **adults** (≈6–8 weeks from ingestion); ♀ lays up to **200,000 eggs/day**.

- **Infective stage:** embryonated egg. **Diagnostic stage:** eggs (or adult worms) in stool. **No secondary host.**
- **Lung phase = larva migrans window** — about 1–2 weeks after infection.

## 4. Pathogenesis and Clinical Features

| Phase | Pathology | Clinical |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intestinal (larval penetration) | Minor mucosal trauma | Often silent |
| **Lung migration** | Alveolar haemorrhage + eosinophilic pneumonitis | **Loeffler syndrome:** cough, wheeze, fleeting pulmonary infiltrates, **marked eosinophilia** |
| Adult worms in gut | Nutrient theft, mechanical effects | Vague abdominal pain, malnutrition in children |
| Heavy worm load | Bolus obstruction | **Intestinal obstruction in children** (ileal region) |
| Aberrant migration | Worms enter ducts or exit orifices | **Biliary/pancreatic ascariasis**, appendicitis, worm vomited/passed per nose |

:::why WHY? — Why do patients cough two weeks after swallowing the egg?
The swallowed larva does NOT stay in the gut: it must complete an obligatory **hepato-pulmonary migration** before it can mature. Larvae breaking into alveoli cause eosinophilic pneumonitis (Loeffler syndrome), which resolves as the larvae ascend and are swallowed again.
:::

## 5. Laboratory Diagnosis

| Step | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Specimen | Stool (fresh) |
| Direct microscopy | Saline wet mount — characteristic mamillated eggs; huge output (200,000/day) makes detection easy |
| Concentration | Formol-ether sedimentation, flotation |
| Kato–Katz | Quantitative thick smear — eggs per gram = worm burden (used in surveys) |
| Other findings | Adult worm passed in stool/vomitus; larvae in sputum during pneumonitis; eosinophilia on CBC |
| Imaging | Ultrasound/ERCP for biliary ascariasis; plain abdomen for obstruction (whirlpool sign of worm bolus) |

## 6. Treatment and Prevention

- **Albendazole** (single dose) or **mebendazole** — benzimidazoles inhibit β-tubulin polymerization of the worm. (Avoid in first trimester of pregnancy per policy.)
- Mass drug administration (MDA) in endemic schoolchildren per national deworming programmes.
- **Prevention:** sanitation (no open defecation), handwashing, washing/cooking vegetables, safe water, health education, periodic deworming.
