## 1. Introduction and Classification

:::definition Exam definition
**Entamoeba histolytica** is a pathogenic **rhizopod (amoeboid) protozoan** inhabiting the large intestine of man, causing **amoebic dysentery** and **extraintestinal amoebiasis** (classically amoebic liver abscess).
:::

- Phylum Sarcomastigophora → class Rhizopoda (moves by pseudopodia).
- Habitat: **large intestine (caecum and ascending colon most affected)**.
- Two forms: **trophozoite** (pathogenic, vegetative) and **cyst** (infective, resistant).

## 2. Morphology — Stage by Stage (Difference-question ready)

### 2.1 Trophozoite (vegetative form)
- 15–30 µm; actively motile with **finger-like pseudopodia** (directional movement).
- Single nucleus with **fine peripheral chromatin** evenly distributed + **central karyosome (endosome)** — "bull's-eye" nucleus.
- **Erythrophagocytosis** (ingested RBCs in cytoplasm) = **pathognomonic of E. histolytica** (distinguishes it from non-pathogenic E. dispar).

### 2.2 Cyst (infective form)
- 10–15 µm, spherical, refractile wall.
- **Mature cyst = 4 nuclei** (immature cysts 1–2 nuclei + chromatoid bodies).
- **Chromatoid bodies** = cigar/sausage-shaped ribosomal aggregates (disappear in mature cysts).
- Passed in **formed stool**; survives outside; resistant to gastric acid.

### Cyst vs Trophozoite (must-know table)

| Feature | Trophozoite | Cyst |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form | Vegetative, motile | Dormant, non-motile |
| Size | 15–30 µm | 10–15 µm |
| Nuclei | One (bull's-eye) | Up to four |
| Found in | Dysenteric (loose) stool, tissue | Formed stool |
| Role | Pathogenic form | **Infective form**; transmission |
| Survival outside | Dies rapidly | Resistant |

## 3. Life Cycle (Simple, single-host)

```flow
Mature 4-nucleated cyst ingested (contaminated food/water)
↓
Excystation in small intestine → 8 metacystic trophozoites
↓
Colonization of large intestine (caecum/ascending colon)
↓
Multiplication by binary fission → invade mucosa (flask-shaped ulcers)
↓
Some trophozoites → encystation in descending colon
↓
Cysts passed in formed faeces → contaminate food/water → next host
```

- **Infective stage:** mature quadrinucleate cyst. **Diagnostic stage:** trophozoites/cysts in stool; trophozoites in abscess aspirate.
- **Dysenteric stool:** trophozoites only (no time to encyst); **formed stool:** cysts.
- Transmission: faeco-oral via cyst carriers (food handlers), water; flies/cockroaches as mechanical vectors.

## 4. Pathogenesis and Clinical Features

### Intestinal amoebiasis

```flow
Trophozoites adhere via GAL/GALNAc lectin
↓
Contact-dependent killing (amoebapores, cysteine proteases)
↓
Liquefactive necrosis of mucosa → FLASK-SHAPED ULCER
(narrow neck through mucosa, broad base in submucosa)
↓
Bloody mucoid diarrhoea (amoebic dysentery)
```

- **Amoebic dysentery:** gradual onset, colicky pain, **blood and mucus** in stool, few stools/day with straining; fever usually absent or low-grade.
- Complications: **toxic megacolon**, perforation/peritonitis, amoeboma (granulation mass mimicking carcinoma), fulminant colitis (steroid-triggered).

### Extraintestinal — Amoebic Liver Abscess (most common)
- Right lobe (≈80%); **"anchovy/chocolate sauce" pus** (necrotic hepatocytes, not true pus — odourless, sterile).
- Fever, tender hepatomegaly, right upper quadrant pain, point tenderness; rupture → lung/pleura/pericardium.

:::why WHY? — Why is the pus called "anchovy sauce"?
The cavity contains liquefied necrotic liver — digested hepatocytes mixed with blood — with few inflammatory cells (trophozoites kill neutrophils). The resulting sterile brown fluid resembles anchovy paste; finding trophozoites in the abscess wall (not the centre) confirms the diagnosis.
:::

## 5. Laboratory Diagnosis (Full Protocol)

| Step | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Specimen** | Fresh warm stool (dysenteric: trophozoites; formed: cysts); liver abscess aspirate; sigmoidoscopic scrapings |
| **Collection/transport** | Examine within 30 min or preserve in PVA/formalin; NO urine/water contamination (free-living amoebae confuse) |
| **Microscopy** | Saline + iodine wet mounts. Trophozoite: directional motility, **ingested RBCs**, bull's-eye nucleus. Cyst: up to 4 nuclei + chromatoid bodies |
| **Concentration** | Zinc sulphate flotation / formol-ether sedimentation for cysts |
| **Staining** | Trichrome / iron haematoxylin for nuclear detail |
| **Antigen detection** | E. histolytica–specific ELISA on stool (distinguishes from E. dispar) |
| **Serology** | IHA, ELISA — **very useful in liver abscess** (>90% positive); may stay positive for years |
| **Molecular** | PCR — most sensitive and species-specific |
| **Culture** | Robinson's medium, xenic; rarely routine |

**Diagnostic algorithm:**

```flow
Dysentery → fresh stool microscopy → trophozoites with ingested RBCs = confirmed
↓
Liver abscess suspected → ultrasound + serology (IHA/ELISA) → aspirate if needed (anchovy pus)
↓
Asymptomatic carrier screening → formed stool cysts / antigen test
```

## 6. Treatment Principles and Prevention

| Situation | Drug class | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invasive disease (dysentery, abscess) | **Metronidazole** (tissue amoebicide) | Follow with a luminal agent |
| Luminal infection / carriers | Diloxanide furoate / paromomycin (luminal amoebicides) | Eradicates cysts; prevents relapse/transmission |

:::why WHY? — Why add a luminal agent after metronidazole?
Metronidazole acts in **tissues** but luminal concentrations are too low to kill cysts remaining in the gut; without a luminal drug the patient stays a carrier and may relapse. Same two-step logic as malaria's "clinical + radical cure".
:::

**Prevention:** sanitation, safe water, food hygiene, treatment of cyst carriers (food handlers), health education. No vaccine.

## 7. High-Yield and Exam Framework

:::highyield High-yield one-liners
- **Infective form = cyst (4 nuclei); pathogenic form = trophozoite.**
- **Ingested RBCs in trophozoite = E. histolytica (pathognomonic).**
- **Flask-shaped ulcer** — narrow neck, broad base, undermined edges.
- Liver abscess: **anchovy-sauce pus**, right lobe, serology positive, aspiration rarely needed.
- Treat invasive disease with metronidazole **then** a luminal agent.
:::
