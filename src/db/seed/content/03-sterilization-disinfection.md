## 1. Definitions (SAQ core — reproduce verbatim)

:::definition Sterilization
The process by which **ALL forms of microbial life, including bacterial spores**, are destroyed or removed. An object is either sterile or not — there are no degrees.
:::

:::definition Disinfection
The destruction of **pathogenic organisms** so that they can no longer cause infection; **spores may survive**. Applied to inanimate objects.
:::

:::definition Antisepsis
Disinfection applied to **living tissues** (skin, mucous membranes) to prevent infection.
:::

:::definition Asepsis
The set of practices that **prevent the entry** of microorganisms into a sterile field or wound ("without sepsis").
:::

| Term | Target | Spores killed? | Applied to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sterilization | All microbial life | Yes | Instruments, media, dressings |
| Disinfection | Pathogens | Not necessarily | Surfaces, instruments |
| Antisepsis | Pathogens | Not necessarily | Living tissue |
| Asepsis | Prevention of entry | — | Wounds, surgical field |

## 2. Classification of Sterilization Methods

```flow
STERILIZATION
↓
PHYSICAL — Heat · Radiation (UV, gamma) · Filtration
↓
Heat splits into MOIST (autoclave, boiling, pasteurization, tyndallization) and DRY (hot air oven, flaming, incineration)
↓
CHEMICAL — gaseous (ethylene oxide, formaldehyde vapour) · liquid (glutaraldehyde)
```

## 3. Moist Heat — the Autoclave (Principle → Procedure → Uses)

:::source The supplied bank asks the autoclave as "principle, procedure and uses" — answer in exactly that order (Rule 82 structure).
:::

### Principle
**Steam under pressure** raises the temperature above the boiling point of water. At **15 psi (≈1.05 kg/cm²)** the chamber reaches **121 °C**, which kills vegetative forms AND **spores** by protein denaturation. Moist heat is far more lethal than dry heat because condensation transfers latent heat and coagulates proteins with much less energy.

### Procedure
1. Load wrapped instruments/media; add water to the correct level.
2. Close the lid; **open the steam tap until all air is expelled** (trapped air gives a false pressure reading).
3. Close the tap; pressure rises to **15 psi = 121 °C**.
4. Maintain the **holding time: 15 minutes** (longer for large fluid volumes).
5. Cool slowly; never open under pressure.

### Controls of the Autoclave

| Control | Type | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browne's tube (type V) | Chemical | Colour change with correct time–temperature |
| Autoclave tape | Chemical | External exposure mark |
| Thermocouple probe | Physical | Actual chamber temperature |
| Spores of *Geobacillus stearothermophilus* | **Biological (gold standard)** | No growth after cycle = sterile |

### Uses
Surgical instruments, dressings, swabs, culture media, contaminated waste, fluids. **NOT** suitable for oils/powders (use hot air oven) or heat-labile items (use filtration or chemicals).

## 4. Other Methods at a Glance

| Method | Parameters | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hot air oven (dry heat) | 160 °C × 2 h holding | Glassware, swabs, oils, powders |
| Flaming | Red heat | Inoculating loops, forceps tips |
| Incineration | — | Dressings, carcasses, contaminated waste |
| Pasteurization | Holder 63 °C × 30 min / **HTST 72 °C × 15 s** | Milk — kills pathogens; NOT sterilization |
| Tyndallization | 100 °C × 20 min on 3 successive days | Media containing spores; exploits germination |
| Filtration | 0.22 µm membrane | Sera, antibiotics, urea — heat-labile fluids |
| Ethylene oxide gas | 45–55 °C with humidity | Plastic disposables, catheters, endoscopes |
| Gamma radiation | Cobalt-60 | Disposable plastics, sutures (industrial) |

## 5. Chemical Disinfectants (Enumeration + examples)

| Group | Example | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alcohols | Ethyl/isopropyl 70% | Skin antisepsis (70% > absolute alcohol) |
| Aldehydes | **Glutaraldehyde 2% (Cidex)** | Cold sterilization of endoscopes, instruments |
| Halogens | Hypochlorites, iodine/iodophors (povidone-iodine) | Water, surfaces, skin |
| Phenolics | Carbolic acid, lysol | Surface disinfection |
| Biguanides | Chlorhexidine | Skin, mucosa, surgical scrub |
| Quaternary ammonium | Cetrimide, benzalkonium | Skin, wounds; useful against Pseudomonas |
| Oxidizing agents | KMnO4, H2O2 | Wounds, water |

:::why WHY? — Why is 70% alcohol better than absolute alcohol?
Water is required to **denature proteins**. Absolute alcohol coagulates surface protein too rapidly, forming a protective shell that prevents penetration; 70% alcohol penetrates fully before coagulation is complete.
:::

## 6. High-Yield

:::highyield High-yield one-liners
- Autoclave = **121 °C, 15 psi, 15 min holding time**; kills spores; biological indicator = *G. stearothermophilus* spores.
- Hot air oven = **160 °C × 2 h**; glassware and powders.
- Pasteurization is **disinfection of milk, NOT sterilization**.
- Filtration for **heat-labile** fluids; ethylene oxide for **heat-sensitive plastics**.
- Glutaraldehyde 2% = cold sterilization of **endoscopes**.
:::
